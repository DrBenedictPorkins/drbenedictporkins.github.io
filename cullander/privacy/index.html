Cullander Privacy Policy

Effective November 3, 2026. Cullander is published by Mike Akram.

Summary

Cullander does not collect any data. Your photos, and everything the app works out about them, stay on your device. The app has no account, analytics, advertising, crash reporting, third-party code or tracking, and sends nothing to the publisher. Photos leave the device only if you turn on Local AI, and then only to the server you enter, on your own network.

What the app does with your photo library

  • With your permission, Cullander reads your photo library on your device to find duplicates, similar shots, blurry photos, screenshots, burst extras and large videos.
  • It uses Apple's on-device Vision framework to label what photos show, to read text in them, and to measure how alike two photos are.
  • The results, including any text read from photos and the piles you sort photos into, are stored on your device only. Deleting the app deletes them.
  • Photos you choose to remove are moved to Recently Deleted by iOS, after iOS asks you to confirm. Cullander does not erase photos itself.
  • Photo and Video Info reads the metadata in a photo or video on your device, such as camera settings and location, and shows it to you. It is not sent anywhere.
  • Compress Video and Keep Still Only save a new video or photo to your library. The original goes to the Trash pile in the app and is removed only when you empty Trash and confirm.
  • Search More Broadly uses Apple Intelligence on supported devices, on the device.
  • Appearance and search filter choices are stored on your device.
  • Albums are created or changed in Photos only at your request.
  • The home screen widget reads a copy of one photo and your progress from storage shared with the app on this device.

When the app uses the network

  • iCloud Photos. When a photo or video has its full-size copy only in iCloud and you open it, play it, compress it, convert it, tap Download from iCloud in Info, or choose Check in iCloud, Photos downloads it from your own iCloud library. Nothing is sent to the publisher.
  • Maps. The Map screen and the map in Photo Info use Apple Maps, which receives the area shown on screen. Photo locations are placed on the map on your device and are not sent.
  • Local AI (off by default). Local AI uses an Ollama server that you run on your own network. When you turn it on, enter an address and tap Start Describing, the app sends a reduced-size copy of each photo to that server, which writes a description. The app refuses addresses outside your local network. Stop ends it, and it does not resume when the app is reopened. The publisher does not receive these photos. The app does not control what your server does with them.
